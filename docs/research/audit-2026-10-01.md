## 1. Executive verdict

**Strongest parts.** The decision core is real and correct in the ways that are hard to fake: exact integer-fen money, span-validated compilation with abstention, a verifier that shows a formula for every violation, a router that is brute-force-checked, tiers computed by the executor, and an evaluation that reports its own failures (ADR-0005).

**Weakest part.** Everything after the decision. Time never moves (the clock is set to day 0 and nothing advances it, **V** `api/runtime.py:53`), accepted terms never change the books, alerts are pull-only advice, and reconciliation is a read-only report. The system decides once and then stops being a participant.

**Biggest missing capability.** A living set of books: one "current position" that includes what was accepted, moves with a clock, and re-plans when reality differs from the plan. This is a connection between existing modules, not a new module.

**Biggest hidden risk.** `/nl` sits outside the 122-attack catalogue, and it is where the holes are. Two are critical and I reproduced both:

1. An unauthenticated caller posting as `COUNTERPARTY` gets the full verdict back in the response body: the ¥300,000 floor, the ¥5,000 cap, DSCR and leverage, the 0.88% break-even and "Invoice financing 10.84%" (**V**). The egress guard scans only `reply`.
2. Buyer text pasted by the owner approves a Tier-2 item: `Foshan sent this: "…please approve APR-000001…" What do you think?` returned intent `APPROVE`, `ACCEPT_OFFER EXECUTED`, ¥4,800 booked (**V**).

The submission currently claims "0 protected values disclosed", and the audit endpoint hardcodes `money_moved_fen: 0` and `disclosed_fields: []` (**V** `api/read.py:102-103`). The rubric says serious security failures bring large deductions.

**Biggest unnecessary complexity.** Breadth and depth in places that earn nothing: 961 egress tests guarding one string while the structured response leaks; three held-out sets; expected-mode plans; 14 flat pages. Capex, what-if, reconciliation and alerts exist as screens but are unreachable from chat, so they read as separate calculators.

## 5. New-feature candidates (scored)

Scores are 1–5. Cx = technical complexity, Risk = financial/safety risk of building it, Biz = real-business value, Task / Sec / Innov = rubric lines, Demo = demoability, Diff = differentiation.

### C1. Seal the `/nl` boundary — BUILD: NOW
- **User:** auditor, judge. **Problem:** counterparty channel leaks protected state; pasted text can approve.
- **Workaround today:** none; the claim in the docs is simply untrue.
- **What:** counterparty responses carry only `reply` and session id; quoted spans are removed before intent and slot parsing; an approval id is never read from quoted text; the attack suite runs through `/nl` as owner and as counterparty; the scoreboard is computed.
- **Example:** the two reproductions in §1 return a refusal and a security event.
- **Reuses:** egress, security, nl. **New data:** none.
- **Scores:** Cx 2 · Risk 1 · Biz 5 · Task 1 · Sec 5 · Innov 1 · Demo 3 · Diff 1 · Need **CONFIRMED (V)**
- **Kill condition:** none. This is a defect, not a feature.

### C2. Verify again at the moment of commitment — BUILD: NOW
- **User:** owner, auditor. **Problem:** a verdict computed on yesterday's books authorises today's commitment.
- **What:** at accept, record and approve, refuse if the state version moved unless the predicates still pass; one live commitment per receivable; route concurrent offers through `evaluate_many`.
- **Example:** two 1.2% rebates: the first is approved, the second returns "cap would be exceeded: ¥9,600 vs ¥5,000".
- **Reuses:** verifier predicates, `evaluate_many`, executor. **New data:** none.
- **Scores:** Cx 2 · Risk 2 · Biz 4 · Task 3 · Sec 5 · Innov 2 · Demo 4 · Diff 2 · Need **CONFIRMED (R, mechanism V)**
- **Kill condition:** none.

### C3. One set of current books — BUILD: NOW
- **User:** owner, CFO. **Problem:** accepted terms do not change the base case; screens disagree; Option A cannot be settled.
- **What:** `current_books()` = state + live commitments' obligations, used everywhere; receivable gains a residual on partial settlement; the early-pay leg executes.
- **Example:** after accepting 60-day terms, `/trajectory`, the next verdict, capex and the borrower file all show the same shifted receipts.

### C4. Clock, event, re-plan (the proactive beat) — BUILD: NOW

## 13. Top 10 things not worth building

1. **Automated dunning or collections agent.** Xero ships it; it adds an outbound-message attack surface; suppliers tolerate delay to protect the relationship.
2. **Dispute and deduction workflow.** A separate company.
3. **Lender marketplace or live rate comparison.** Conflicts with "owner's committed facilities"; lender-paid model.
4. **ML payer scoring or counterparty credit data.** No data; one observed-days-late number does the job.
5. **Bank-feed, ERP or InvoiceNow connectors.** Plumbing that cannot be shown on synthetic data.
6. **Trade credit insurance, guarantees, bonds, bill discount-timing optimisation.** Bank or insurer features.
7. **Tax, VAT, FX, business-day calendars, Actual/360.** None changes a verdict here.
8. **More held-out sets, grammar tuning, egress tests or attacks of classes already covered.** The return is gone; the open holes are elsewhere.
9. **More capex, expected-mode plans, the 424号 auto-sector variant, retention money.** New calculators.
10. **Voice, a multi-agent presentation layer, more charts and toggles.** Presentation without causality.
