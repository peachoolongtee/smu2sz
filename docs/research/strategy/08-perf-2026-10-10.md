# Envelope and worth read times on the public-record books (map #58, ticket #78), 10 October 2026

Books: `fixtures/scenarios/public_sme.json` read through the pilot runtime (`scripts/pilot_runtime.py`), the clock advanced by
the dry pilot's own steps; mandate `public_sme.mandate.json`. Machine: the lead's Mac, shared with other work (load average 3
to 6 while these ran), so seconds move by about 10 percent between runs; the counts do not. "Before" is `dev` at bbfbdb4
(the same engine and strategy code the dry pilot ran), "after" is this branch. The three routes were read through the app with
the flags on, `zone=1` for the envelope as the runbook reads it, one buyer at a time, cold (the answer cache and the
verifier's caches empty).

## 1. What dominates

**The planner, nothing else.** One envelope read for Buyer A (`zone=1`, week 1, day 7) runs 73 distinct settings through
`verifier.evaluate` and 124 router searches (`Search.run`): 98 percent of the 19 seconds is inside `Search._search`
(profile: 50.7 of 51.3 s). Everything the strategy module does besides (building offers, reading verdicts, wording) is under
a second. So the lever is how many evaluations a read asks for, never what one costs (`app/funding`, `app/verifier` are
not touched).

| Phase of Buyer A's read (day 7, `zone=1`) | settings | seconds |
|---|---|---|
| the six levers (terms, price, early pay, rebate) | 19 | 3.8 |
| the zone (one setting per terms value) | 12 | 2.7 |
| the package grid (3 terms values x early-pay variants x price x rebate) | 42 | 12.4 |

Inside the grid, 36 of the 42 settings were the rebate searches of its six cells (six or seven settings each).

**Which calls.** Per evaluation: one committed-plan search (0.08 to 0.2 s); an early payment adds the plan for the books
without it (0.2 s more); a refused offer adds the verifier's counters, which are more router searches (a price-adjustment
counter alone is 16 searches, 3.5 s over the read). In the 61 settings of a `zone=0` read: 60 committed-plan searches
(4.7 s), 29 early-payment comparisons (5.7 s), 16 price-counter searches (3.5 s), 4 other counter searches (0.9 s).

**Can the single-flight cache hold per-buyer partial results across the terms grid?** No, for a plain reason: no two grid
points ask the planner the same question. The bound obligations carry ids and source spans that come from the offer
(`WHATIF-<hash>-O1`, character offsets into the offer's text), and the settings differ in terms, price, discount or rebate
besides. Holding one router-run memo (`shared_runs()`) open for the whole sweep saved 2 of 124 searches. The verifier's own
cross-evaluation caches (baseline, comparison) already catch what repeats. The `AnswerCache` is keyed by the books, the
buyer and `zone`, so a `zone=1` read after a `zone=0` read starts again; that is real but is a different saving (see 4).

**Does the worth ranking recompute the plan per receivable?** The standing plan, no: it is shared inside the read's
`shared_runs()`. What it recomputes is the rest of the books' reading: each of the 21 receipts it measures
(`marginal_impact`) re-read the books as they stand (trajectory and alerts) before reading the books with the receipt. The
cost is elsewhere: 8 early-payment ceilings (whole invoice, the plan's need, the verifier's counter face) took 46
comparisons, each a router search plus a projection (8.0 s of 12.6 s), because the rate search bisected from the top of a
60 to 150 bp grid for a boundary that sits at 5 to 10 bp on these books; the 21 receipts took 3.6 s. In the weeks with no
plan (ticket #79) the 25 receipts of week 8 each searched an infeasible plan, 0.85 s each: 21 of the 22 seconds.

## 2. What changed (strategy modules only)

1. **A search reads a verdict without the counters.** `_Verifier.quick` asks the verifier for the verdict with
   `with_counters=False` (the verifier builds counters only for a refused offer; `evaluate` offers no switch, so
   `verifier.evaluate._evaluate` is called, and a refused setting's engine error is still worded by the public `evaluate`).
   A setting that passes has no counters either way and its probe goes to the memo as it is; a refused setting is kept
   apart and, if the answer shows it (a "next step", the counters of an ask), is evaluated whole on the router its first
   reading already searched. Passing and refusing are the same under both readings (`tests/strategy/test_search_economy.py`).
2. **The rebate hint survives rounding.** The hint (where the forward-concession budget runs out) required the verifier's
   totals to be a whole number of fen per 5 bp step; they never are, so it was never used and every cell searched the rebate
   from nothing. It now divides exactly (`Fraction`); a wrong hint still falls back to the search, so it only saves.
3. **The terms levers ask the top of the range once.** With `top_first`, a lever whose first and last values both pass is
   read as passing throughout (two evaluations instead of five for a 7-value range). The early-payment maximum and the
   collections' early-payment ceiling are searched from the bottom of their rate grids (boundaries sit at 5 to 10 bp).
4. **The worth ranking reads the books as they stand once** (`impact.read_books`, `marginal_impact(before_reading=...)`), and
   with no committed plan it reads no hypothetical receipts at all (`effects` is empty; the saving of a receipt is not priced
   against nothing, and each one cost close to a second). That is the one payload change besides ticket #79's own.

No displayed figure moved: for every route on all six buyers and the curve and worth routes, the JSON of days 0, 7, 14, 35
and 56 was diffed before and after (computed_in_seconds and evaluations removed); the only differences are the new
`no_plan` field (false) on the curve and worth answers on days 0 and 7, and `effects` emptied on the worth rows of days 14,
35 and 56 (no plan). `fixtures/strategy/*`, `tests/strategy`, the day-34 fixtures and `strategy_backtest.py --quick` pass.

## 4. What would take the rest

* Read `zone=0` for the weekly read and `zone=1` only when the owner opens the zone (the web already does this): week 1 is then
  6.9 to 8.9 s for all four buyers. The runbook and `pilot_dry_run.py` read `zone=1` today.
* Key the cache by the books and the buyer and hold the zone apart, so a `zone=1` read after a `zone=0` read adds only the
  zone's 2 to 3 s.
* If `zone=1` must also fit in 10 s for B and C, the remaining cost is about 40 independent evaluations of 0.2 to 0.4 s each
  (the terms levers, the zone, the descend checks): they can run in parallel worker processes (the planner is pure Python, so
  threads do not help), a change in how the app runs rather than in the strategy modules; or the planner can be told that
  settings which differ only in an obligation's id or a rebate's rate are the same question (a change under `app/funding`,
  stability mode).

## 5. After the follow-ups (branch `fix/pilot-zone-read`)

The weekly envelope read in `scripts/pilot_dry_run.py` and in the runbook is now `zone=0`; the zone table is read only
with `--zone` (into `envelope-<buyer>-zone.json`) or on demand. The route caches the sweep and the zone table apart
(`strategy_envelope._cache` and `_zone_cache`, both by the books, the mandate, the day, the buyer and the commitments), so a
`zone=1` read after a `zone=0` read adds only the table.

Week 1 (day 7), public-record books, cold, through the route, one buyer after another on a shared machine (load 3.7 to 5.1);
the `zone=1` answers are byte-identical to the one-shot `zone=1` answers of section 3 (computed_in_seconds and evaluations
left out):

| Buyer | `zone=0` read | then `zone=1` (adds the table only) | `zone=0` + table | one-shot `zone=1` before this change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 6.8 | 3.1 | 9.9 | 9.0 to 10.4 |
| B | 8.1 | 2.7 | 10.8 | 10.2 to 12.9 |
| C | 7.7 | 3.3 | 11.0 | 11.7 to 12.8 |
| F | 7.5 | 3.1 | 10.6 | 9.8 to 10.7 |

The weekly read, now without the table, is 6.8 to 8.1 s for all four buyers; reading the table too costs 2.7 to 3.3 s more.
